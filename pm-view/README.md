# Prediction-market view

The desk's calls on Polymarket markets, each written down with the market's own probability before the desk's and scored by Brier against it when the market settles. Started 2026-11-03 under the prediction-market programme ([`../RESEARCH_AGENDA.md`](../RESEARCH_AGENDA.md), 2026-11-01); it replaces the rates desk view ([`../desk-view/`](../desk-view/)) once that view's open calls settle on 2026-12-31. Until then both run.

Why it is built this way: the rates view's first call, V1, was made against the market with no stated reason why the market was wrong, and missed ([`../predictions/POST_MORTEMS.md`](../predictions/POST_MORTEMS.md)). The rules adopted then (baseline before confidence; a departure needs a testable reason; Brier against the baseline every time) are simpler on a prediction market, because the baseline is the market: the mid of the best bid and ask on the YES token is a probability. So:

1. **The market first.** `fetch.py quote <market id>` reads the market record and the YES token's order book, keeps the raw bodies git-ignored with their hashes in `data/manifest.json`, and prints the mid, spread, volume, scheduled end and whether the market is eligible for a call (the same floors as Study 013's §6.3: binary, order book open, resolution not begun, two-sided book, spread at most 0.10, mid inside [0.01, 0.99], at least 10,000 USDC traded). The desk writes its own probability only after that line exists.
2. **A call needs a departure and a reason.** A call is logged only when the desk's probability differs from the market mid by at least 0.10, and its basis names the market's number and says in one testable sentence what the market is missing. Nearer than that the desk has nothing to add and says so in the view instead of logging a call. Enforced on the committed ledger by `tests/test_ledger_discipline.py`. **A market is described from its rules, not its title** (2026-11-04, after the first view called two touch markets "price-level markets", [`../predictions/POST_MORTEMS.md`](../predictions/POST_MORTEMS.md)): the quote prints the market's own resolution text under the quote line and records its hash; the call's `settles_on` restates that rule with its source and the level or date; the view's table carries the same sentence beside each market's title. A call whose `settles_on` is missing, has no number, or repeats the proposition is refused by the same test. **On a barrier market the method's own bias and range come before the threshold** (2026-11-04, after the first live run of `barrier.py`, [`../BUILD_LOG.md`](../BUILD_LOG.md) 12:15Z): a reading below the market is never a call, because a daily-close method under-reads a touch, so a market above it is at least as consistent with the bias as with a mispricing; a market mid between the primary and the sensitivity readings is never a call, because the reading's own window range covers it; only then does the 0.10 threshold apply, on the primary window alone. `barrier.py` prints the verdict in that order beside each departure.
3. **Scored on the venue's settlement, once.** `score.py` scores each due call by the outcome rule Study 013 pre-registered ([`../research/013-polymarket-calibration-forward/PREREGISTRATION.md`](../research/013-polymarket-calibration-forward/PREREGISTRATION.md) §6.5, `src/outcome.py`, imported unchanged): YES when the UMA status is `resolved`, the settlement prices are 1 and 0 and the CLOB winner flags agree; NO on the reverse; a fractional settlement or a disagreement closes the call as excluded, never scored. Both Brier scores, the desk's and the market mid's, go in the row. A call is never scored twice; the ledger is never edited.
4. **Skill, not hit rate.** `score.py --summary` writes `SKILL.md`: desk and market Brier over the scored calls and their difference. With fewer than about thirty scored calls the figure is descriptive.

Files:

- [`PM_VIEW.md`](PM_VIEW.md): the current view, dated; what was read, what was called or why not, the running score.
- `ledger.csv`: one row per call, **append-only**: `id`, `made_on` (UTC), `venue`, `market_id`, `condition_id`, `yes_token_id`, `proposition` (the desk's own words for what YES means), `settles_on` (the market's resolution rule restated: source and the level or date; added 2026-11-04), `due_on` (the market's scheduled end date), `market_mid`, `market_spread`, `volume_usdc` (all three from the quote line), `desk_p`, `basis`, `quote_sha256` (the book body's hash in `data/manifest.json`).
- `outcomes.csv`: created by the first scoring run; `id`, `scored_on`, `kind`, `y`, `desk_p`, `market_mid`, `brier_desk`, `brier_market`, and the hashes of the two settlement records read.
- [`SKILL.md`](SKILL.md): the running score.
- `pm_lib.py`: the pure functions (book touch, eligibility in §6.3 order, the call rule, Brier; the rules excerpt; `touch_probability` and `realised_vol` for the barrier markets: first passage of a driftless lognormal price, tested against the closed form and a one-minute simulation); `fetch.py`, `score.py` as above; `tests/`.
- `barrier.py`: the touch-market method on the FRED series CBBTCUSD (Coinbase Bitcoin, daily), end to end: fetch (raw git-ignored, hashed into `data/fred_manifest.json` with dates and counts, never a value), realised volatility on a **pre-specified** primary window of 30 observations (the month the market spans) with 90 as a reported sensitivity, horizon to the end of the Eastern-time month, a daily close at or beyond a barrier inside the month counts as touched, zero drift, the spot level printed only on request (one dated level per view). Pre-specified and committed 2026-11-04 before the series was first read, so the window could not be chosen to make or avoid a call. It prints the departure from a given market mid against the 0.10 threshold; it never writes a call.
- `calibrate.py`: the touch method scored on its own history before it is used in a view (pre-specified and committed 2026-11-05 before the first run on the series): every complete Eastern-time month of the kept FRED download, barriers at log distances ±0.025 to ±0.20 from the first in-month close, predictions from the 30- and 90-observation windows, outcome a later daily close at or beyond the barrier, a climatological baseline (the touch rate over earlier months only, at least twelve), Brier per window and per barrier, reliability tables, a month-block bootstrap of the differences, and three rules fixed in the file: R1 which window is the primary, R2 whether the method beats the baseline and may be a basis for a call at all, R3 its direction of error in the bins the live readings sit in. Writes aggregates only, to `data/calibration.json`. Limit: the daily-close vs one-minute-extreme gap is not measurable from this series.
- `data/manifest.json`: one entry per quote (time, ids, hashes incl. the resolution text's, the market's title, the computed numbers). `data/fred_manifest.json`: one entry per fetch of the price series (time, URL, bytes, SHA-256, row count, first and last observation dates, citation). `data/raw/` is not committed.

**Data terms.** Polymarket's verdict is `derived` ([`../tools/pm_data_terms.md`](../tools/pm_data_terms.md)): no raw response is committed. A call row carries the market's identifiers and three numbers the desk computed from the book at the time of the call (mid, spread, cumulative volume), and the view quotes the same; the bodies stay in `data/raw/`, hashed. Reads are public, no key, no wallet, read-only, inside the documented rate limits, with a declared user agent. Paper only: no order, no wallet, no account.

Run from this folder with the repository's virtualenv: `python fetch.py quote <ids>`, `python score.py [--dry-run]`, `python score.py --summary`, `python -m pytest -q`.

Nothing here is investment advice. The calls are written to be falsified, and some will be.
