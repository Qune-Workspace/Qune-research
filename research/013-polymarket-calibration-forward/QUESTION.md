## 1. The question, the sources and the test object

**Decision: calibration of Polymarket prices, in the favourite-longshot direction, tested on binary markets that resolve after the date the pre-registration fixes, at one fixed horizon before resolution, by a logistic recalibration slope.** The question:

> **Over Polymarket binary markets that resolve after the pre-registration date, is the price at a fixed horizon before resolution compressed towards 0.50 relative to the outcome frequency (logistic recalibration slope b > 1: longshots overpriced, favourites underpriced), and does the compression hold once Sports markets are set aside?**

This is the first of the scope's research questions as the agenda restated it on 2026-11-01 ([RESEARCH_AGENDA.md](../../RESEARCH_AGENDA.md)): calibration and the favourite-longshot bias, by category, time to resolution and liquidity. Cross-venue divergence (question 2) is parked until a second venue's terms allow it.

**Sources.**

- **[CR26]** Cardozo, Marcos, and José Ignacio Rivero-Wildemauwe, "The Favorite-Longshot Bias in Prediction Markets: Evidence from Polymarket", arXiv:2609.12878, September 2026 (v1 of 2026-09-11), read in full from the arXiv HTML on 2026-11-02 00:1x-00:3xZ. Data: version 1.3 of the "Polymarket Users" dataset, 588 million trades by 2.48 million wallets, 2022-11-11 to 2026-03-29; 729,133 child markets in 316,429 parent events, of which 614,883 child markets in 255,427 parent events record a trade. What it reports (quoted or restated from its text): purchases below 10 cents lose 6.30 percent per dollar when child markets are weighted equally (95% interval −8.38 to −4.22, clustered by parent event), 19.35 percent when purchases are pooled (interval −46.99 to +8.30), and earn +4.09 percent when parent events are weighted equally; favourites (at or above 90 cents) earn 0.277 / 0.83 percent. In probability units (outcome share less mean price, "probability error") the longshot tail is +0.24 / −0.23 / +0.20 percentage points under the three aggregations, and within the tail "does not exceed 0.53 percentage point in absolute value". Across the interior of the price range (its Figure 1, equal child-market weights, bins of 5 cents): "From the bin beginning at 4 cents through the bin beginning at 45 cents, every probability error is negative ... From 50 cents through 99 cents, every probability error is positive. The confidence intervals exclude zero in each of these bins. The magnitudes, however, are largest away from the tails. The probability error reaches −5.86 percentage points in the bin beginning at 35 cents and +7.28 percentage points in the bin beginning at 60 cents." By category: "the two-sided pattern is robust in Crypto and Politics but surprisingly absent in Sports"; Sports and Crypto hold 86.9 percent of the child markets with longshot purchases. Its object is the purchase (price paid, token count, wallet), not the market at a horizon.
- **[WA26]** Walker, Aidan, "The Price is (Almost) Right: A Calibration Analysis of Polymarket Pricing Data", Princeton University senior thesis (Operations Research and Financial Engineering; adviser Daniel Rigobon), 2026-04-09, handle 88435/dsp01mw22v8973. **Abstract only:** the full text answered HTTP 401 ("Authentication is required") on 2026-11-02 00:20Z at both bitstream routes, and was not read. What the abstract reports: 188,509 resolved binary markets and 1,875,570 daily price observations, 2022-11 to 2025-12; Brier skill score 0.398 overall, 0.264 in Sports to 0.565 in Politics; "The fitted overall logistic recalibration slope b = 1.112 confirms broad underconfidence consistent with the well-documented Favorite-Longshot Bias (FLB): prices are systematically compressed toward 0.50, with longshots overpriced and favorites underpriced. This bias is universally present across all market domains and worsens at longer time horizons"; an event-level clustered bootstrap widens the intervals and "two horizon bands lose statistical significance". The slope is the market-level object this study tests; its figure is used here as reported from the abstract and is re-read from the text if the text becomes readable, with a dated addition.
- The older literature the two sources build on (Griffith 1949; Thaler and Ziemba 1988; Snowberg and Wolfers 2010; Page and Clemen 2013 on Intrade) is known to the author from the sources' citations and from general knowledge, not read here; none is added to the agenda's register until read.

**Primary trial C1.** The sample: every Polymarket binary market (two outcome tokens; negative-risk children included, each as its own market; markets that resolve 50/50 excluded) with an order book, whose venue resolution time `closedTime` falls after the pre-registration's commit date **D** and inside its collection window, and whose UMA status is `resolved`. For each market, **p** is the YES-token price at the fixed horizon **h** before `closedTime`, taken from the desk's daily snapshot of the open-market listing (the last snapshot at least h before `closedTime`; the pre-registration fixes h, the snapshot field and the minimum liquidity), and **y** = 1 if the YES token won. Outcome rule, fixed now from the desk's 2026-11-01 field checks (outcome fields read by the desk on 90 markets that resolved before this note, none of which can enter the sample): `outcomePrices` on a UMA-`resolved` market, cross-checked against the CLOB's `tokens[].winner`; a disagreement excludes the market and is counted. The test fits

  logit Pr(y = 1) = a + b · logit(p)

by maximum likelihood with standard errors clustered by parent event, and decides on **H1: b > 1** against **H0: b ≤ 1** (b = 1 and a = 0 is a calibrated market; b > 1 is compression towards 0.50, the direction both sources report). The intercept a is reported, with no decision weight. The reliability table (ten price bins, outcome share less mean price, clustered intervals) is reported beside b for the reader, with no decision weight, so that the result can be read against [CR26]'s Figure 1 and [WA26]'s diagrams.

**Why a slope and not a tail return.** [CR26] shows that in return units the longshot tail has a per-market standard deviation of order sqrt((1 − p)/p) (about 4.4 at p = 0.05) and that its sign depends on the aggregation; in probability units the tail effect is a few tenths of a percentage point, which no forward window can see ([`tools/power_pm013.txt`](../../tools/power_pm013.txt), bucket rows). The compression is largest in the interior of the price range (−5.86 and +7.28 points in [CR26]'s interior bins), and the slope uses every market at once. The slope is also the object [WA26] published a figure for, so the power ratio can be stated in its units.

Not registered: a return-unit tail test (above), a Brier skill score against a constant (descriptive, reported without decision weight), a strategy that fades longshots (scope question 5 follows only from a result here; nothing is sized or traded in this study).

**Family count.** A new family: no earlier study of this programme used prediction-market data. N = 3 for the deflated criterion where it applies; the programme-wide trial count, re-counted from every `research/*/trials.md` immediately before the run, is reported beside the result.

## 2. Power, from counts only ([`tools/power_pm013.txt`](../../tools/power_pm013.txt))

The script fetched the venue's closed-market keyset listing for one scheduled end date in every five from 2026-08-01 to 2026-10-30 (19 windows, 6,757 pages; 100 rows a page, cursor pagination; the offset form refuses deeper pages), kept a whitelist of fields per market and counted by calendar month of `closedTime`. Nothing fetched is published; the kept records are git-ignored under `tools/data/raw/` with a page manifest of SHA-256 hashes. A month's count is a lower bound: a market that resolved early under a scheduled date after the last window was not fetched.

Fetched 2026-11-02 00:55–01:09Z: 674,505 distinct closed markets kept with whitelisted fields (the first version of the script held every record in memory and was killed by the host at about 7.6 GB at 00:50Z with nothing written; a streaming version replaced it). Every market fetched is UMA-`resolved`, has an order book and carries `resolutionMechanism` oracle. By `closedTime` month the sample holds 143,345 (August), 223,493 (September) and 295,586 (October) markets, of which 104,576 / 186,598 / 257,424 carry a Sports tag: the venue's listing is dominated by short-dated sports and price markets, most of them below the volume floor.

Monthly rates, total over the 19 windows / 19 × 30.44 days (a lower bound): markets 1,080,539; distinct parent events 165,578; "clean" markets (UMA-resolved, order book, volume ≥ 10,000 USDC) 42,377; distinct parent events among clean markets 24,292; the same without a Sports tag 14,866.

*Correction, recorded before any figure was copied here:* the script's first output (01:09:37Z) divided the sampled total by the 38 distinct `endDate` day labels instead of the 19 fetched windows (a window also returns the markets scheduled exactly at the next midnight, which carry the next day's label), so every rate was understated by half and every MDE overstated by √2. Fixed at 01:2xZ; the denominator now comes from the page manifest; the docstring of `tools/power_pm013.py` records it.

**Minimum detectable departure of the slope from 1.** Under H0, SE(b̂) = 1 / sqrt(n · E[x² p(1 − p)]) with x = logit(p) and E over the price distribution at the horizon, which is not known before the snapshots exist; three assumptions are computed (uniform on 0.02–0.98: E = 0.2159; U-shaped: 0.2542; half the markets in the two tails: 0.2927), and the data step measures the distribution from the snapshots before any outcome is read and recomputes the row, as Study 005 measured σ_d at its data step. MDE = K · SE at K = 2.842 (t ≥ 2.0 at 80% power, the programme's convention) and 2.486 (one-sided 5% at 80% power). The published effect is [WA26]'s b − 1 = 0.112.

| window | independent unit | n | MDE on b − 1 (uniform E, K = 2.842) | ratio to 0.112 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 w | distinct parent events, clean | 22,347 | 0.0409 | 0.37 |
| 4 w | the same, non-Sports | 13,676 | 0.0523 | 0.47 |
| **8 w** | **distinct parent events, clean** | **44,694** | **0.0289** | **0.26** |
| 8 w | the same, non-Sports | 27,352 | 0.0370 | 0.33 |
| 12 w | distinct parent events, clean | 67,041 | 0.0236 | 0.21 |
| 12 w | the same, non-Sports | 41,027 | 0.0302 | 0.27 |

Under the U-shaped and tails-heavy assumptions the MDEs are 8–15% smaller; the one-sided K = 2.486 column and the market-count rows are in the power file. The bucket form cannot see the published tail effect in any window (ratios 1.4–5.5 against 0.24 points); the interior bins can at twelve weeks (ratios 0.29–0.64 against 5.86 points), which is why the reliability table is reported and not decided on.

**Decision row and window, fixed now.** The decision row is C1 at **eight weeks** of resolution dates, with the independent count taken as distinct parent events among clean markets (24,292 a month; 44,694 in eight weeks) under the uniform price distribution, the most conservative of the three E assumptions: MDE 0.0289 on b − 1, ratio **0.26** of [WA26]'s 0.112. C1a on the same row without Sports: n = 27,352, MDE 0.0370, ratio 0.33. Both run as tests (gate: at most 0.50). Four weeks would pass too (0.37 and 0.47) but leaves C1a near the limit once the liquidity floor at the horizon removes markets; twelve weeks buys little (0.21) for a month's delay. The window is eight weeks of `closedTime` from the first snapshot date S that the pre-registration records (S ≥ D); if the data step's measured price distribution or its counts move the recomputed ratio above 0.50, the study runs as a labelled low-power check and says so.

**What the ratio does not say.** [WA26]'s 1.112 was fitted on daily observations pooled across horizons and the whole 2022–2025 history; at one fixed horizon the slope may be nearer 1 (the abstract says the bias worsens with horizon, so a one-day horizon is the conservative choice for finding it) and the 2026 market is larger and more liquid than the one it measured (inferred). The effective count assumes one independent observation per parent event; markets in different events that share an underlying (the same match, the same price series) are correlated too, and the clustered standard error does not see that. The counts are a lower bound for the reason above and an upper bound for the test because the liquidity floor, the 50/50 exclusions and markets without a snapshot at the horizon will remove some.

## 3. What the author knows before choosing, and the snapshot plan

### 3.1 Known before any data

- [CR26]'s results as quoted in §1, read in full, including its bin-by-bin probability errors and its category table; [WA26]'s abstract figures.
- The desk's data tasks of 2026-11-01 (field checks, private): the outcome fields of 90 markets resolved in 2024 and 2026 were read by the desk to establish which field is authoritative (`outcomePrices` is set before UMA finality; the Gamma `uma_resolution_status` query filter is not honoured, so UMA status is filtered client-side; CLOB `tokens[].winner` agreed on 20 of 20 UMA-resolved markets). Those markets resolved before this note and cannot enter the decision sample.
- The count probes of this run (no outcome, no wording): one scheduled day holds more than a thousand closed markets; the 2026-10-01 probe's 100 rows sat in 75 parent events, all UMA-resolved, all with an order book, median volume about 68,000 USDC.
- General knowledge: the author's training covers Polymarket markets and outcomes up to its cutoff, so any market resolved before the pre-registration is not out of sample for the author ([METHODS §4](../../METHODS.md#4-what-these-guards-do-not-do)); that is why the decision sample is markets resolving after D, and why no historical row carries decision weight.

The author has not read the outcome of any market in the decision sample, because none has resolved; has not fetched any price at any horizon for any market; and has not seen the wording of any market in the count records (the script never writes it).

### 3.2 Snapshots, resolution and vintage

- **Daily snapshots.** From the day the collector is built and tested (before D), the desk fetches the open-market listing once a day at a fixed time (Gamma `/markets/keyset`, `closed=false`, every page; the listing's `bestBid`, `bestAsk`, `lastTradePrice`, `liquidityNum`, `volumeNum`, `endDate`, parent event ids and tag labels; the listing is cached up to five minutes, stated by the venue), hashes each page in a manifest, and keeps the rows git-ignored. Nothing is backfilled: a market with no snapshot at the horizon is not in the sample, and the count is reported.
- **Resolution.** After `closedTime` + the UMA challenge period, the market's record is fetched again; a market enters the sample only when its UMA status is `resolved` and the two outcome sources agree. Markets still unresolved at the end of the scoring window are counted, not filled.
- **Vintage.** The venue can change a listing's metadata; the snapshot of record is the one taken, with its hash, and the note says so. There is no second download of the price at the horizon.
- **Terms.** Polymarket `derived`: no raw row is committed; the published files are the slope table, the reliability table and the counts, computed by the desk, attributed to Polymarket with the fetch dates. The verdict is re-read, and `tools/pm_data_terms.md` dated again, before the pre-registration is committed.

## 4. Why not the alternatives

- **A historical test on 2022–2026.** Mined twice ([CR26] to 2026-03-29, [WA26] to 2025-12) and known to the author; a run on it would be a replication with nothing at stake. If the pre-registration keeps a small historical slice as a pipeline check, it is labelled a replication and carries no decision weight.
- **Cross-venue divergence (scope question 2).** Parked: Kalshi is `neither`; Manifold and PredictIt terms unread.
- **Information speed around scheduled news (question 3).** Needs event timestamps and intraday prices aligned to them; a later study, after the snapshot collector has run.
- **A tail-return test or a fade-the-longshots rule.** Return units cannot be powered (§1); a rule follows only from a calibration result, under scope question 5.
- **Reading [WA26]'s full text through another route.** Not attempted beyond the two public bitstream routes; no sign-up, no credential.

## Question gate

- Power ratio: 0.26 of the published slope departure from 1 (MDE 0.0289 against [WA26]'s b − 1 = 0.112, abstract figure) on the decision row of tools/power_pm013.txt: 8 w, distinct parent events among clean markets, uniform E, K = 2.842, n = 44,694; C1a (non-Sports) on the same row 0.33.
- Persistence: longshot demand on the venue is recurrent and not borne by a group that learns ([CR26] §6: the top decile of past longshot buyers supplies 26.6 percent of the next month's purchases below 10 cents and earns the same as other buyers), fees and capital locked until resolution make the correcting trade costly against a favourite return under one percent per dollar, and [WA26] found the compression in every domain and horizon to 2025-12; the rule fails against its benchmark (a calibrated market, b = 1) if post-2025 liquidity has removed the compression at a one-day horizon, or if the sample is carried by Sports, where [CR26] found no two-sided pattern, which C1a is registered to show.
- Data terms: Polymarket's Terms of Use effective 2026-08-11, read on 2026-11-01 (tools/pm_data_terms.md, verdict `derived`): aggregates computed by the desk may be published with attribution, no raw response is committed; the snapshot rows and the kept count records are withheld (git-ignored, hashed in manifests) and a reader regenerates them by running the committed collector and tools/power_pm013.py against the public API; the verdict is re-read before the pre-registration.
