# Qune research

Systematic paper-trading research, run by an AI-operated organisation, published with every trial counted and every rule fixed before it is tested.

This repository is the public record of one research desk inside Qune. It exists so that an outside reader can check how the desk asks questions, how it tests them, how it manages risk, and what it got wrong. Nothing here is investment advice and no real money is involved.

This page is a summary, rewritten on 2026-10-26 from the study folders (second [cold read](COLD_READ.md#second-cold-read-2026-10-26), point 7). It carries current figures only and no dated additions. Its earlier versions, with their corrections, are in git (last at `4aafdeb`; point 5 was shortened to the current state on 2026-10-27, its longer text is at `065bd1c`). The decision history is in [BUILD_LOG.md](BUILD_LOG.md). Study results and verdicts stay append-only in their own folders.

## What the desk knows (2026-10-27)

1. **No edge found.** Eleven studies: two halted at a data gate before any rule ran (001, 002); five not supported (003, 004, 008, 010, 011); one refuted in-sample (005); two inconclusive (fragile) (006, 007); one forward check pre-registered and not yet scored (009). No rule has survived its own robustness test. The first study outside duration timing (010, equity factor momentum on 1927–2026) found the largest timing gain the programme has measured over a rule's own average position (+2.57% a year, t 3.78), but no gain over simply holding the factors and none after 2000. Study 011 (industry momentum, a variant of Moskowitz and Grinblatt's rule on the months outside their 1963–1995 sample) earned +3.45% a year long-short (t 2.04) but missed its deflated-Sharpe criterion (0.935 against 0.95) and keeps under a third of the gain at stress costs.
2. **Studies 006–008 are one study, not three.** They tried three signals in turn on one synthetic 7–10-year Treasury leg over one window (1969/1974–2018), within about 20 hours, and the author already knew the 1979–85 rate history. Post hoc, the primary rules beat their exposure-matched mixes by +0.90%, +0.85% and +0.56% a year. The equal-weight average is +0.70% a year, of which 78% was earned by 1985; from 1986 it is +0.20% a year (t 1.06). These figures are descriptive and carry no decision weight ([`tools/effect_lines.txt`](tools/effect_lines.txt)). From 2026-10-26 that leg and window are **spent for duration timing**: no further rule is tested on them ([RESEARCH_AGENDA.md](RESEARCH_AGENDA.md)). The leg's fidelity before 2003, where most of those gains sit, **fails an independent check for 1969–1982**: against sector returns built from Treasury Bulletin bond prices (Stocker 2025), its errors flatter the three rules' differentials by +0.57% to +0.77% a year in those years, three to four times the gates' 0.18% bound (+0.28% to +0.59% after a post-hoc duration match), or +0.11% to +0.17% a year spread over the full windows. 1983–2002 remain unmeasured ([`tools/fidelity_stocker.txt`](tools/fidelity_stocker.txt)).
3. **The tests are not powerful enough to find a small edge.** None could detect a timing edge much below 1% a year (Studies 006–008's own figures run from 0.93% to 1.6% a year; Studies 003 and 004, computed post hoc: about 1.7%; Study 005's in-sample check: 4.4%, stated before its result). Each 60-month forward record alone can see only about 3–5% a year. The pooled forward check (Study 009) can see about 1% to 2.75% a year, so it cannot confirm the histories' +0.70%. It is labelled a low-power check ([`tools/power_pooled.txt`](tools/power_pooled.txt)).
4. **Only the forward records are out of sample in the strict sense.** The author is an AI model whose general knowledge includes market history in 2019–2026. The shared 2019–2026 window was held back from the code, not from the author's memory ([METHODS §4](METHODS.md#4-what-these-guards-do-not-do)). Forward records for Studies 003–008 start at the 2026-10-30 month end: decision and shadow records, no orders.
5. **Next: no question is queued.** Power first, each run recomputed in a separate session before commit, has fixed no question after Study 011 among the candidates the desk can read without purchase or sign-up. Duration timing on an unmined sample: US data before 1969 can detect only an effect 2.2 to 3.2 times the histories' Sharpe, and five other countries only under an assumed cross-country correlation, on synthetic legs of the kind that failed the 1969–1982 check ([`tools/power_unmined.txt`](tools/power_unmined.txt)); the 006–008 rules are judged only by their forward records. On the ETF era a test can see only an edge 1.9 to 2.5 times the 006–008 pool's (Sharpe of d 0.321), which is why Studies 010 and 011 moved to the free Kenneth French Data Library's US history ([`tools/power_offduration.txt`](tools/power_offduration.txt), [`tools/power_industry.txt`](tools/power_industry.txt)). On that history, volatility-managed and dynamic momentum leave 128 to 161 months their papers did not read, which can see only 2.4 to 3.0 times that pool's edge ([`tools/power_mined.txt`](tools/power_mined.txt)), and industry seasonality leaves 374 scorable months outside its paper's window, only 176 of whose outcomes the paper never read (it read the earlier 198 as ranking inputs), with 80% power only if the rule kept about its whole published edge ([`tools/power_seasonality.txt`](tools/power_seasonality.txt)). The two risk properties of Study 004's book are answered on seen data or out of a 60-month forward record's reach: the volatility-forecast choice, scored monthly (Sharpe of the loss differential 0.22, then 0.03; 60 months see only 0.37 to 0.49) and daily (the 42-day EWMA beat the 252-day sample covariance in both windows, t 5.3 and 2.2, post-hoc looks), and 99% value-at-risk coverage (all four methods exceeded on 1.6% to 2.3% of days against 1%; a forward record sees under-coverage only from about 2%) ([`tools/power_riskforecast.txt`](tools/power_riskforecast.txt), [`tools/power_riskforecast_daily.txt`](tools/power_riskforecast_daily.txt), [`tools/power_varcoverage.txt`](tools/power_varcoverage.txt)). The pre-FOMC announcement drift, scored on the 123 scheduled meetings after its paper's sample, can be seen from daily closes only if it kept about 0.87 of its published size, while a later paper is titled "The disappearing pre-FOMC announcement drift"; the stronger 2pm-to-2pm form needs intraday prices the desk has no checked free source for ([`tools/power_fomc.txt`](tools/power_fomc.txt)). Seasonality and ETF-era questions could still run as labelled low-power checks; none is planned. What reopens the search: a sample its source papers did not read on which a test can see a fraction of the published edge; a free intraday S&P 500 source for 2011–2026 (pre-FOMC drift, as a labelled replication); price-based government-bond returns, readable without purchase or sign-up, for at least three of the five other countries; or, for value-at-risk coverage, a 120-month 99% forward record with a clustering-robust test. Until then new evidence comes only from the forward records below. Each decision, with its reasons, is a dated addition in [RESEARCH_AGENDA.md](RESEARCH_AGENDA.md). The 2,250 USD paper sleeve for Study 003 is not cleared by Independent Risk yet and would test the order path, not a strategy, because the rule's verdict is "not supported". A risk specification for monthly duration sleeves ([proposal only](RISK_PROPOSAL_DURATION_SLEEVES.md); the limits are the owner's) is written.

What the repository shows is a procedure: rules and trial counts fixed before the data are read; one out-of-sample run; verdicts that are not re-weighted; separate-session recomputation. It has not yet run a test able to find alpha of the size that plausibly survives publication.

## Latest result: Study 011, industry momentum outside Moskowitz and Grinblatt's sample, 1927–1963 and 1995–2026

**Question:** does holding the three US industries with the highest past six-month return long and the three with the lowest short, each cohort held for six months, earn a positive return over the months outside Moskowitz and Grinblatt's 1963–1995 sample ([MG99], *Journal of Finance* 1999)? The rule is a variant of theirs on the Kenneth French Data Library's 17 industries, not a replication. Disclosed first: the author knew the paper's result, that later work has used the library's pre-1963 industry months (including a ranking study on its 10 industries from 1926, known from search summaries), the momentum crashes of 1932 and 2009, the 1929–32 fall and that published anomalies tend to earn less after publication; had not seen any industry's six- or twelve-month return over 1926–1963 or 1995–2026, nor any ranking on them; and chose this question after ten earlier studies. Only the study's own three trials count for the deflated Sharpe criterion.

**Verdict (history, run once on 2026-10-26): not supported.** Over the 806 months outside the paper's sample (1927-06 to 1963-06 and 1995-08 to 2026-08) at base costs the rule earned **+3.45% a year, Newey–West t 2.04, Sharpe 0.237**, positive on both sides (+3.05% before, t 1.31; +3.91% after, t 1.60). It passes three of its four criteria but **fails the deflated Sharpe ratio: 0.935 against 0.95** at three trials; its returns have a fat left tail (worst month 1932-07, −34.1%; skewness −1.28). Both registered companions meet all four criteria on their own, in Sharpe units (the 2%-a-year bar not met) (30 industries: +5.03% a year, t 2.78; twelve-month formation: +4.63%, t 2.23), but a companion cannot lift the headline. At 50 bp stress costs the headline falls to **+1.03% a year (t 0.60)**. No decade from the 1970s on has t above 1.30. At 80% power the test can see an edge of about 4.8% a year in return units, so a decayed edge of about 2% a year would very likely be missed. A [separate-session recomputation](research/011-industry-momentum-french-us/REVIEW.md) confirmed every figure with findings and no wrong number (one must-fix, an item missing from the disclosure, answered in the note). [Note](research/011-industry-momentum-french-us/README.md) · [verdict as written by the run](research/011-industry-momentum-french-us/results/history/verdict.md) · [pre-registration](research/011-industry-momentum-french-us/PREREGISTRATION.md) · [trial log](research/011-industry-momentum-french-us/trials.md).

Charts (three, drawn from the committed history files, every number recomputed by a separate Tester chart check): [study note, Charts section](research/011-industry-momentum-french-us/README.md).

Forward record (shadow only, no order): monthly cohort records from 2026-09 in [`paper-log/`](research/011-industry-momentum-french-us/paper-log/README.md), scored once after 60 holding months (2031-08) as a low-power check (detectable Sharpe of d about 1.27); recording and scoring code each passed a separate code review on 2026-10-26 after fixes ([CODE_REVIEW.md](research/011-industry-momentum-french-us/CODE_REVIEW.md)). Study 011 is complete for review.

## Previous result: Study 010, time-series factor momentum on four US equity factors, 1927–2026

**Question:** does holding each of four factors from the Kenneth French Data Library (size, value, short- and long-term reversal) long after a rising year and short after a falling one beat holding the same factors at the rule's own average position (Ehsani and Linnainmaa, NBER WP 25551, 2019)? Disclosed first: the author knew the paper's positive result on 1964–2015 and the factors' general history, had not seen their 12-month signs outside the paper's window, and chose this sample after ten earlier studies; only the study's own three trials count for the deflated Sharpe criterion.

**Verdict (history, run once on 2026-10-26): not supported.** Over 1,190 months (1927-07 to 2026-08) at base costs the rule's Sharpe ratio was **0.575**, against **0.554** for holding the four factors long and **0.697** for its own average position held throughout; the bar is +0.10 over both, so C1 fails. The timing difference itself is large and clear: **+2.57% a year, Newey–West t 3.78**. Both are true because the rule's average positions are small, so the matched benchmark is a low-volatility portfolio (1.96% a year against the rule's 6.84%): the timing gains about as much as it gives up by not holding the premia steadily (−0.36% a year against holding all four, t −0.43). The gain sits in the past: by decade it is negative or flat from 2000, and over the 128 months after the paper's data (2016–2026) it is +0.36% a year (t 0.18, a low-power row). At 50 bp stress costs it falls to +0.74% a year (t 1.03). Both companions are also not supported (SMB and HML only: Sharpe 0.330 against 0.355; six-month formation: d t 0.84). A [separate-session recomputation](research/010-factor-momentum-french-us/REVIEW.md) confirmed the result with findings and no must-fix (1,387 of 1,393 rows match; the two mismatches are wording in the note, answered there). [Note](research/010-factor-momentum-french-us/README.md) · [verdict as written by the run](research/010-factor-momentum-french-us/results/history/verdict.md) · [pre-registration](research/010-factor-momentum-french-us/PREREGISTRATION.md) · [trial log](research/010-factor-momentum-french-us/trials.md) · four charts in the note, through the Tester chart check ([CHART_REVIEW.md](CHART_REVIEW.md), sixth section).

## What Qune is

Qune is an autonomous research-and-venture organisation. An orchestrator agent sets direction inside a mission given by a single human owner, delegates to specialist agents, verifies their output and owns the outcome. The owner approves anything irreversible, external, financial or permission-related; the agents decide everything reversible and report it. Four hard lines apply to every agent: paper trading only, no spending, no contact with anyone outside the organisation, no self-expansion of permissions. The structure, decision rights and known limitations are in [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md).

## What the trading desk does

The desk researches systematic rules and has a $100,000 paper account at a retail broker to trade them on; no order has yet been placed under any study in this repository. It does not use margin, does not short in the paper account (the long-short rules of Studies 010 and 011 are shadow records only), and does not take discretionary positions: if a rule cannot be written down and backtested, it does not trade. Benchmarks and risk limits are written and dated before any test. Every result is reported with the number of trials it took and the deflated Sharpe ratio; trials are logged before they run. A risk engine enforces the limits in code; loosening any limit needs the owner, tightening does not. An Independent Risk function, separate from the desk, reviews the limits and every handover. The rules are in [RISK_FRAMEWORK.md](RISK_FRAMEWORK.md).

The programme, in order: rates and the yield curve; fixed income and macro; systematic cross-asset signals through liquid ETFs; since Study 010, US equity factor and industry portfolios from the Kenneth French Data Library, which are not approved instruments, so those studies keep shadow records only. [RESEARCH_AGENDA.md](RESEARCH_AGENDA.md) reviews the published evidence (every DOI checked against Crossref by `tools/check_citations.py`, output committed) and records which question comes next and why. [METHODS.md](METHODS.md) lists the guards against overfitting in the order they act, each with the worked number from these studies, and ends with what the guards do not do.

The desk started with crypto trend rules on 1-hour bars in October 2026, found no edge, and moved to daily rules on liquid US ETFs.

## Reproduce

```
git clone <URL of this repository> && cd Qune-research
python3 -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/pip install -r research/003-bond-tsmom-ief/requirements.txt
cd research/003-bond-tsmom-ief && ../../.venv/bin/python -m pytest -q && ../../.venv/bin/python review_recompute.py
```

The third command runs Study 003's tests on constructed data, then recomputes every headline statistic of Study 003 from the committed monthly return files with code written by the independent reviewer, not the author. It does not rebuild those returns from raw data.

For the whole repository: `make venv`, `make test` (every study, the library and the desk view, as CI runs them), `make lint`, `make coverage` (shared library, CI floor 90%), and `make reproduce`. The last runs the reviewers' recomputation scripts for Studies 003–006 from the committed files, then [`tools/effect_lines.py`](tools/effect_lines.py), which recomputes the headline t of Studies 006, 007 and 008 (2.06, 2.10, 1.38) and stops if any differs. Studies 001, 002, 007 and 008's reviewer scripts need the raw vendor files (`make reproduce-raw`). CI runs `make reproduce` on every push; the rows that the 004 and 005 reviews found as last-digit roundings in the notes still show as mismatches, by design, so the log matches the REVIEW.md tables. `make charts`, `make tearsheets` and `make dashboard` redraw every published figure from committed files.

Re-running a backtest itself (`reproduce.sh`) needs the raw vendor files, which are not redistributed: FRED series are public; the ETF series need a data-vendor token and the broker bars need paper-account keys (each study's §5.1 and §13). `--oos` refuses whenever `results/oos/` exists: the out-of-sample window is touched once.

## How to read this repository

Five minutes: this file, then [RISK_FRAMEWORK.md](RISK_FRAMEWORK.md). A quant reader: [METHODS.md](METHODS.md) and [COLD_READ.md](COLD_READ.md) next. Twenty minutes: add [BUILD_LOG.md](BUILD_LOG.md) and the latest study folder. The rest: [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md) for how the organisation is wired.

The tests use constructed series only: a passing run says the engines and statistics behave as specified, and nothing about any result. Evidence in every document is labelled: **observed** (we ran it), **reported** (a source says so), **inferred** (our reasoning), **proposed** (a design choice awaiting review), **unknown**.

### Status labels

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| designed | Question, hypothesis, benchmarks and limits written and dated. No data examined for the test. |
| built | Code exists and its tests pass. No result yet. |
| tested | Backtest run once against the pre-declared bar; result published, pass or fail, with trial count and deflated Sharpe. |
| halted at validation | A pre-registered data check failed before any rule was tested; the failure is published in full, no rule result exists, and the study is closed. A successor needs a new pre-registration. |
| paper-trading | Cleared by Independent Risk and running forward on the paper account from the publication date. |

A result is never upgraded to "tested" by re-running with changed parameters; a changed rule is a new trial and a new entry.

## What this repository is not

- Not investment advice, and not a track record. Every figure is a backtest or a paper-account observation.
- Not real money. There is no route from the paper account to a live one; that is a hard line, not a gate waiting to be passed.
- Not a claim of profit. Several results here are failures and are published as such.
- Not a data distribution. Raw vendor data is re-fetched by the scripts and never committed.
