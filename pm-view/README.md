# Prediction-market view

The desk's calls on Polymarket markets, each written down with the market's own probability before the desk's and scored by Brier against it when the market settles. Started 2026-11-03 under the prediction-market programme ([`../RESEARCH_AGENDA.md`](../RESEARCH_AGENDA.md), 2026-11-01); it replaces the rates desk view ([`../desk-view/`](../desk-view/)) once that view's open calls settle on 2026-12-31. Until then both run.

Why it is built this way: the rates view's first call, V1, was made against the market with no stated reason why the market was wrong, and missed ([`../predictions/POST_MORTEMS.md`](../predictions/POST_MORTEMS.md)). The rules adopted then (baseline before confidence; a departure needs a testable reason; Brier against the baseline every time) are simpler on a prediction market, because the baseline is the market: the mid of the best bid and ask on the YES token is a probability. So:

1. **The market first.** `fetch.py quote <market id>` reads the market record and the YES token's order book, keeps the raw bodies git-ignored with their hashes in `data/manifest.json`, and prints the mid, spread, volume, scheduled end and whether the market is eligible for a call (the same floors as Study 013's §6.3: binary, order book open, resolution not begun, two-sided book, spread at most 0.10, mid inside [0.01, 0.99], at least 10,000 USDC traded). The desk writes its own probability only after that line exists. **A settled market is quoted from its record, not reported as a failed fetch** (2026-11-06, after the 80,000 touch market, resolved on 4 November, came back as `FAILED book fetch failed: HTTP Error 404` and the record the quote already held was thrown away, [`../predictions/POST_MORTEMS.md`](../predictions/POST_MORTEMS.md) "A settled market reported as a fetch failure"): the record's part of the eligibility check runs before any book is read; a market the record excludes is quoted from the record with its settlement fields (`closed`, `closedTime`, `umaResolutionStatus`, `outcomePrices`) printed and written to the manifest as `settlement`, no book is read, exit 0; a book that fails on an open market is still a failed quote.
2. **A call needs a departure and a reason.** A call is logged only when the desk's probability, a probability strictly inside (0, 1) `[gate:probability]`, differs from the market mid by at least 0.10 `[gate:departure]`, and its basis names the market's number and says in one testable sentence what the market is missing `[gate:basis-names-market]`. Nearer than that the desk has nothing to add and says so in the view instead of logging a call. Enforced on the committed ledger by `tests/test_ledger_discipline.py`. **A market is described from its rules, not its title** (2026-11-04, after the first view called two touch markets "price-level markets", [`../predictions/POST_MORTEMS.md`](../predictions/POST_MORTEMS.md)): the quote prints the market's own resolution text under the quote line and records its hash; the call's `settles_on` restates that rule with its source and the level or date; the view's table carries the same sentence beside each market's title. A call whose `settles_on` is missing, has no number, or repeats the proposition is refused by the same test `[gate:settles-on]`. **On a barrier market the method's own bias and range come before the threshold** (2026-11-04, after the first live run of `barrier.py`, [`../BUILD_LOG.md`](../BUILD_LOG.md) 12:15Z): a reading below the market is never a call `[gate:touch-below]`, because a daily-close method under-reads a touch, so a market above it is at least as consistent with the bias as with a mispricing; a market mid between the primary and the sensitivity readings is never a call `[gate:range]`, because the reading's own window range covers it; only then does the 0.10 threshold apply, on the primary window alone `[gate:method-departure]`. `barrier.py` prints the verdict in that order beside each departure. **A method is scored against climatology on its own history before it may be a basis for a call** (2026-11-05, `calibrate.py`, [`../BUILD_LOG.md`](../BUILD_LOG.md) 12:15Z): on 126 complete months of the FRED series the touch method's Brier (0.2171 on the 30-observation window, 0.2170 on 90) is not below the climatological touch rate's (0.2147; month-block bootstrap of the difference [−0.015, +0.019]), so under rule R2 of the calibration, fixed before the run, `barrier.py` begins every verdict with "no call" `[gate:calibrated]` and prints the reading beside the market and beside the climatological rate at the nearest pre-specified distance; the two windows do not differ (R1: 30 stays). The same run found that on its own outcome the method *over-reads* a touch by 0.108 in the 0.5–0.9 bins (R3), so the "known downward bias" this rule asserted on 2026-11-04 was an assumption about the one-minute gap, never measured; the net sign of the method's error against the market's rule is not known and the basis sentence says so. The gate lifts only when a re-run of `calibrate.py` under the same rules reports R2 true, dated. **The bill reading of the FOMC event was scored the same way, and passes where the touch method failed** (2026-11-05 18:2xZ, `fomc_calibrate.py`, [`../BUILD_LOG.md`](../BUILD_LOG.md) 18:15Z): on 74 scheduled meetings 2017-07..2026-10 the reading's three-class Brier (0.298 net, 0.396 raw) is below climatology's (0.529) and persistence's (0.622) with both year-block intervals below zero, so under its R2 the reading may be a basis for a call `[gate:calibrated]`. Two limits stand beside that: in the 0.4–0.8 bin, where the 3 November reading of 0.58 sat, the reading over-read on four meetings (a hike followed one), and in the 0.9–1.0 bin a hike followed on 15 of 19; the reading is informative at its ends and thin in the middle, and the view prints the frequency in the reading's own bin beside it. **The reading's range comes from the basis's history, not from the day's basis** (same run, after the first live run: on 3 November the 4-week bill sat exactly on funds, the day's NET–RAW range collapsed to a point, and a rule written an hour earlier would have passed a 0.36 departure through): the range is the reading under the 5th and 95th percentile of the 4-week bill minus funds over the last 252 observations; a market mid inside it is never a call `[gate:range]`; then the 0.10 threshold on the primary `[gate:method-departure]`. `fomc_calibrate.py --live` prints the reading, the range, the market and the verdict in that order and never writes a call. **The committed ledger is held to every rule above, not to the first one** (2026-11-06 00:15Z, [`../predictions/POST_MORTEMS.md`](../predictions/POST_MORTEMS.md) "A call gate that knew one rule of three"): until this date `tests/test_ledger_discipline.py` checked the departure and `settles_on` only, while the R2 gate and the range rule lived in `barrier.py` and `fomc_calibrate.py` as printed advice, and no tool writes a call, so a hand-written row could have passed every test while breaking two of the three rules. Now a call names its `method` (`fomc_bills`, `btc_touch`, or `stated` for a reason outside any method) `[gate:method-named]` and, for a method call, carries `method_p`, `range_lo` and `range_hi`, the range bracketing the reading `[gate:range-brackets]`; `pm_lib.method_violations` is the one copy of the gate (R2 from the method's committed calibration file `[gate:calibrated]`, the market outside the range `[gate:range]`, a touch reading above the market `[gate:touch-below]`, the 0.10 threshold on the reading `[gate:method-departure]`, the desk on the reading's side of the market `[gate:desk-side]`), the ledger test applies it to every row, and the two printers are tested to agree with it on a grid and print the four fields on a candidate line so they are copied, not retyped. `stated` carries no gate beyond the departure and the row says so `[gate:stated-bare]`. *Every rule in this paragraph carries the tag of the check that enforces it* (2026-11-06 12:15Z, [`../predictions/POST_MORTEMS.md`](../predictions/POST_MORTEMS.md) "A rule in prose, a check elsewhere: the mechanical search"): the `[gate:…]` tag beside a rule is the tag `pm_lib.GATE_TAGS` puts in front of the refusal the ledger's gate gives for that rule, and `tests/test_rule_tags.py` holds three lists equal, the tags in this file, the tags the gate's source names and the tags the gate emits when driven over rows that break each rule, and requires every bold rule in this paragraph to carry at least one. A rule added in prose without its check, or a check without its prose, fails a test instead of waiting for a reader; this is the class of defect found three times in the week before (`../research/POST_MORTEMS.md` "A refusal fixed in the newest copy only", 31 October, and "A leak scan that read what it could never publish", 5 November; the ledger gate above, 6 November). The search's first run found one more: `barrier.py` passed a reading through to "a call needs a basis sentence" when no calibration file was given, where the gate refuses; the printer now refuses too and the grid test covers that state.
3. **Scored on the venue's settlement, once.** `score.py` scores each due call by the outcome rule Study 013 pre-registered ([`../research/013-polymarket-calibration-forward/PREREGISTRATION.md`](../research/013-polymarket-calibration-forward/PREREGISTRATION.md) §6.5, `src/outcome.py`, imported unchanged): YES when the UMA status is `resolved`, the settlement prices are 1 and 0 and the CLOB winner flags agree; NO on the reverse; a fractional settlement or a disagreement closes the call as excluded, never scored. Both Brier scores, the desk's and the market mid's, go in the row. A call is never scored twice; the ledger is never edited.
4. **Skill, not hit rate.** `score.py --summary` writes `SKILL.md`: desk and market Brier over the scored calls and their difference. With fewer than about thirty scored calls the figure is descriptive.
5. **Every method reading is recorded beside its market and scored, as information** (2026-11-06 18:15Z, [`../predictions/POST_MORTEMS.md`](../predictions/POST_MORTEMS.md) "A week of readings that no record could score"): a call is rare by design, so the ledger alone would take years to say whether a method's reading carries information against the market. `readings.py add` admits one row per method, market and view day to `readings.csv`, built from the written quote in `data/manifest.json` (ids, mid, scheduled end and hash copied, never retyped) and the printer's candidate line (reading, range, climatological probability where one was on record), with the call gate's refusals for the same numbers in its `gate` column so the record also says why each reading was not a call. `pm_lib.reading_violations` is the gate, applied where the row is admitted and again by `tests/test_readings.py` on the committed file: a method with a calibration file, a range that brackets the reading, the mid equal to the quote's, the quote written on the reading's day with the market eligible. **A reading made on a `--dry-run` quote has no hash and is not on the record**: on the day this rule was written it kept three of the week's five readings off the file, among them the one case that had already settled (the 80,000 touch market, read 0.607 against 0.805 three hours before it resolved YES on 4 November); a view's readings are made on written quotes from now on, and `--dry-run` is for testing the tool. `score.py` scores each reading once, when its market's scheduled end has passed or a later written quote carries a settlement, by the same §6.5 rule as a call, into `readings_outcomes.csv` with the Brier of the reading, the market mid and the climatological probability; `SKILL.md` carries the per-method table under the calls and says it is information, not a call. The due register lists due readings.

Files:

- [`PM_VIEW.md`](PM_VIEW.md): the current view, dated; what was read, what was called or why not, the running score.
- `ledger.csv`: one row per call, **append-only**: `id`, `made_on` (UTC), `venue`, `market_id`, `condition_id`, `yes_token_id`, `proposition` (the desk's own words for what YES means), `settles_on` (the market's resolution rule restated: source and the level or date; added 2026-11-04), `due_on` (the market's scheduled end date), `market_mid`, `market_spread`, `volume_usdc` (all three from the quote line), `desk_p`, `method`, `method_p`, `range_lo`, `range_hi` (the method the call rests on, its reading and the range the gate is tested on; added 2026-11-06, empty for `stated`), `basis`, `quote_sha256` (the book body's hash in `data/manifest.json`).
- `readings.csv` (added 2026-11-06): one row per method reading per market per view day, **append-only**: `id`, `made_on` (the quote's day), `recorded_utc`, `venue`, `market_id`, `condition_id`, `yes_token_id`, `proposition`, `method`, `method_p`, `range_lo`, `range_hi`, `market_mid` (from the quote), `climatology_p` (the method's baseline where on record), `due_on`, `gate` (the call gate's refusal tags, or `open`), `quote_fetched_utc`, `quote_sha256` (the quote's Gamma hash), `source` (where the reading was published). `readings_outcomes.csv`: created by the first scoring run; `id`, `scored_on`, `kind`, `y`, `method_p`, `market_mid`, `climatology_p`, the three Brier scores and the two settlement hashes.
- `outcomes.csv`: created by the first scoring run; `id`, `scored_on`, `kind`, `y`, `desk_p`, `market_mid`, `brier_desk`, `brier_market`, and the hashes of the two settlement records read.
- [`SKILL.md`](SKILL.md): the running score.
- `pm_lib.py`: the pure functions (book touch, eligibility in §6.3 order, the call rule, Brier; the rules excerpt; `touch_probability` and `realised_vol` for the barrier markets: first passage of a driftless lognormal price, tested against the closed form and a one-minute simulation); `fetch.py`, `score.py` as above; `tests/`.
- `barrier.py`: the touch-market method on the FRED series CBBTCUSD (Coinbase Bitcoin, daily), end to end: fetch (raw git-ignored, hashed into `data/fred_manifest.json` with dates and counts, never a value), realised volatility on a **pre-specified** primary window of 30 observations (the month the market spans) with 90 as a reported sensitivity, horizon to the end of the Eastern-time month, a daily close at or beyond a barrier inside the month counts as touched, zero drift, the spot level printed only on request (one dated level per view). Pre-specified and committed 2026-11-04 before the series was first read, so the window could not be chosen to make or avoid a call. It prints the departure from a given market mid against the 0.10 threshold; it never writes a call.
- `calibrate.py`: the touch method scored on its own history before it is used in a view (pre-specified and committed 2026-11-05 before the first run on the series): every complete Eastern-time month of the kept FRED download, barriers at log distances ±0.025 to ±0.20 from the first in-month close, predictions from the 30- and 90-observation windows, outcome a later daily close at or beyond the barrier, a climatological baseline (the touch rate over earlier months only, at least twelve), Brier per window and per barrier, reliability tables, a month-block bootstrap of the differences, and three rules fixed in the file: R1 which window is the primary, R2 whether the method beats the baseline and may be a basis for a call at all, R3 its direction of error in the bins the live readings sit in. Writes aggregates only, to `data/calibration.json`. Limit: the daily-close vs one-minute-extreme gap is not measurable from this series.
- `fomc_calibrate.py`: the bill-implied FOMC reading (the method of the 2026-11-03 view: 3-month bill minus funds, net of the 4-week bill's basis, divided by the policy content of one step at the meeting, `predictions/baseline.py::max_policy_spread`) scored on the scheduled meetings since 2016 before it may be a basis for a call on the December 2026 event (pre-specified and committed 2026-11-05 18:22Z, f066f88, before the four series were first read for it): reading date 35 calendar days before each decision (a Friday view's position before 9 December), NET the primary and RAW the sensitivity (the pair the view printed as 0.58 and 0.79), a three-class forecast (hike, no change, cut) against the change in the target range's upper bound within a week of the meeting, baselines climatology (class frequencies over earlier meetings only) and persistence (the previous meeting's class), three-class Brier, year-block bootstrap, and rules R1 (primary), R2 (beats both baselines or is no basis for a call), R3 (direction of error in the 0.4–0.8 bin where the live reading sits) fixed in the file. A 7-day secondary is reported only. Writes aggregates only, to `data/fomc_calibration.json`; the four FRED series (DTB3, DTB4WK, EFFR, DFEDTARU) stay git-ignored under `data/raw/fred_rates/`, hashed into `data/fred_rates_manifest.json`. `--live <meeting> --mid hike=<mid> --mid none=<mid>` prints the current reading from the kept download with the calibration, the range under the historical basis, the market and the verdict beside it; it never writes a call.
- `data/manifest.json`: one entry per quote (time, ids, hashes incl. the resolution text's, the market's title, the computed numbers). `data/fred_manifest.json`: one entry per fetch of the price series (time, URL, bytes, SHA-256, row count, first and last observation dates, citation). `data/raw/` is not committed.

**Data terms.** Polymarket's verdict is `derived` ([`../tools/pm_data_terms.md`](../tools/pm_data_terms.md)): no raw response is committed. A call row carries the market's identifiers and three numbers the desk computed from the book at the time of the call (mid, spread, cumulative volume), and the view quotes the same; the bodies stay in `data/raw/`, hashed. Reads are public, no key, no wallet, read-only, inside the documented rate limits, with a declared user agent. Paper only: no order, no wallet, no account.

Run from this folder with the repository's virtualenv: `python fetch.py quote <ids>`, `python readings.py add ...`, `python score.py [--dry-run]`, `python score.py --summary`, `python -m pytest -q`.

Nothing here is investment advice. The calls are written to be falsified, and some will be.
