# Post-mortems of the desk's calls

One entry per scored miss or near-miss, newest first. Each ends in a rule, a test or a changed procedure, and a line in [`../BUILD_LOG.md`](../BUILD_LOG.md). The ledger is never edited; the lesson goes here and into the method.

## A settled market reported as a fetch failure (written 2026-11-06, 06:15Z run, the first weekly view)

**What happened (observed, 06:16:38Z).** The weekly view's first quote of the four markets printed, for the Bitcoin 80,000 touch market (6507378), `FAILED book fetch failed: HTTP Error 404: Not Found`, and `fetch.py` exited 1. The market had settled: Polymarket's record showed `closed` true, `closedTime` 2026-11-04 15:16:24 UTC, UMA status `resolved`, outcome prices 1 and 0, so the YES token's order book no longer existed and the CLOB answered 404. `quote_one` had already read the record, which said all of that, and threw it away: it fetched the book before it applied the record's own part of the eligibility check, treated the book's absence as a failed fetch, wrote nothing (not even the record it held), and reported the market as unread. A run that trusted the line would have written "fetch failed" into the view for a market that had settled against the method's reading two days earlier, and the run's exit code would have called the quote a failure of the network rather than a fact about the market.

**Why it was there.** `eligibility` was written in Study 013's §6.3 order, which puts the record's conditions (binary, order book, open and accepting orders, resolution not begun, scheduled end) before the book's (two-sided, spread, mid, volume), but `quote_one` called it once, at the end, with both inputs; the book fetch sat in between, and a book fetch can only fail. Every market quoted until today was open. The first settled market was the first time the order of the reads mattered, and it mattered on the market whose settlement is the view's one case against the method's rule (0.607 read three hours before a YES against a market of 0.805).

**Changes (built and tested 06:1x–06:2xZ, before the view was written; 65 pm-view tests, ruff and mypy clean).** `pm_lib.record_eligibility(row, at)` is the record's part of the check, in the same order, and `eligibility` calls it first. `fetch.quote_one` applies it before any book is read: a market the record excludes is quoted from the record, with `pm_lib.settlement_state` printing the record's own fields (`closed`, `closedTime`, `umaResolutionStatus`, `outcomePrices`) as information, the gamma body and the manifest entry written with `settlement`, no book file, exit 0. A book that fails on an open market is still a failed quote, exit 1, and the test holds both. The re-quote at 06:20:45Z is the view's row. The scoring rule for a call stays Study 013 §6.5 in `score.py`; the quote line reports what the record says and decides nothing about an outcome.

**The rule.** A tool that reads in sequence reports what the earlier read already established before it calls the later one failed; "failed" is reserved for a read that should have succeeded. In this folder: the record decides first, the book decides the rest, and a market that has settled is a quote with a settlement line, not an error.

**Still unknown.** Whether the other reader of the same two endpoints, `score.py`, has the same shape (it reads the Gamma record and the CLOB market record, not the book; a 404 there is an excluded call with its kind written, by design). Whether a settled market's record keeps its `closedTime` form; the test pins the field names the record showed today.

## A call gate that knew one rule of three (written 2026-11-06, 00:15Z run, before the first Friday view)

**The defect (observed, found by reading the code six hours before the view that would have used it).** README rule 2 of the prediction-market view states three conditions for a call: a departure of at least 0.10 with a basis that names the market (2026-11-03), the method's calibration gate R2 (2026-11-05 12:15Z: a method that does not beat its dumb baseline on its own history is no basis for a call) and the range rule (2026-11-04 and 2026-11-05 18:15Z: a market mid inside the reading's own range is never a call; on a touch market a reading below the market is never a call). The committed ledger was held to the first condition only. `tests/test_ledger_discipline.py` called `pm_lib.call_violations`, which knew the departure, the basis word and `settles_on`. The R2 gate and the range rule lived in `barrier.departure_verdict` and `fomc_calibrate.live_verdict`, two printers whose docstrings say "never writes a call". No tool writes a call: a ledger row is typed by hand from the printers' output. So a row on Friday carrying the bill reading of 0.993 against the market's 0.635, departure 0.358, would have passed every test in the folder, although the printer had said "no call: the market mid lies inside the reading's range [0.596, 1.000]"; and a row on the touch markets would have passed with the method's R2 false. The ledger could not refuse either.

**How it happened.** The three rules were added on three days by three runs, each fixing the rule where the run was looking: the first in the ledger's gate, because the ledger was the only artefact then; the next two in the method scripts, because the runs were building the methods and the scripts were where the readings appeared. Each run tested its own rule in its own file. Nobody asked where a call is finally admitted and whether that place knew every rule, which is the question the 31 October refusal fix (`research/POST_MORTEMS.md`, "A refusal fixed in the newest copy only") and the 5 November scan fix ("a check reads exactly what the action it guards can touch") had each already answered once in another folder. The defect is the same class a third time: a rule stated in prose and enforced in the one copy that happened to be open.

**Why it matters now.** The first weekly view runs at 06:15Z today with the first reading that passes R2 (the bills) and the first reading that fails it (the touch formula) side by side. The two cases the ledger could not refuse are exactly the two cases the day would present.

**Changes (built and tested 2026-11-06, 00:2x–00:4xZ, before the view):**

1. *One copy of the gate.* `pm_lib.method_violations(method, method_p, range_lo, range_hi, market_mid, r2, desk_p)` returns, in the README's order, what stops a method from being a basis for a call: the method is one the desk has; `stated` carries no reading and no range; R2 of the method's committed calibration is true (missing: refused); the range brackets the reading inside [0, 1]; the market mid lies outside the range; for the touch method the reading is above the market; the reading departs from the mid by at least 0.10; the desk's probability sits on the reading's side of the market. `pm_lib.method_gate` reads R2 from the method's own calibration file and nothing else (`METHOD_CALIBRATION`); `call_violations` refuses a call with no method and runs the gate for one with.
2. *The ledger carries what the gate needs.* Four columns after `desk_p`: `method` (`fomc_bills`, `btc_touch`, `stated`), `method_p`, `range_lo`, `range_hi`. The ledger test loads both calibration files, types the four columns and applies `call_violations` with the gate to every row; a second test asserts that both files are committed, that each carries a boolean R2, and records their values on this date (bills true, touch false), so a gate flipped by a re-run is a visible change.
3. *The printers agree with the gate.* `barrier.departure_verdict` and `fomc_calibrate.live_verdict` keep their wording and now print the four ledger fields on a candidate line (the FOMC range as the hull of the reading and its historical-basis range), so a row is copied, not retyped. Two grid tests hold them to `method_violations`: on 13,718 touch points (19³ readings × sensitivities × mids under both R2 states) and over 10,000 bill points (ranges, the reading at either end or the middle, mids, R2 true, false and no file) the printer says "a call needs a basis sentence" or "call candidate" exactly when the gate is empty. Six new tests and four extended; the folder's 58 became 64.

**The rule.** A rule about an action is enforced where the action is admitted, and the check there knows every rule stated about it; a printer that advises is tested to agree with that check. Before a view that could write the first row of a ledger, the run reads the ledger's test and asks what it cannot refuse.

**Still unknown.** Whether the `stated` path, which carries no gate beyond the departure, will be used honestly; it exists because the README names a reason from the data releases as a legitimate basis, and the row shows plainly that no method stands behind it. Whether a fourth copy of a rule exists somewhere the desk has not looked: the mechanical search for this class (a rule in prose, a check somewhere else) has not been written and is the next candidate.

## PM view 2026-11-03: two markets described from their titles, not their rules (written 2026-11-04)

**The miss.** The first prediction-market view ([`../pm-view/PM_VIEW.md`](../pm-view/PM_VIEW.md), 2026-11-03 18:23Z) named the monthly Bitcoin markets 6507378 and 6507380 as "price-level markets" and proposed "a barrier probability from realised volatility (the method of the rates view's V4)" as the stated method for a call on them. Their rules, read six hours later from the Gamma records ([`../tools/pm_data_terms.md`](../tools/pm_data_terms.md), 2026-11-04 00:17Z) [Q], settle YES if *any* Binance BTC/USDT one-minute candle in the Eastern-time month has a High at or above 80,000 (a Low at or below 75,000). They are touch markets. No call was made, so nothing was scored; the miss is a near-miss of the method, caught by the terms read before any number was written.

**What the desk read, and what it did not (observed).** The view's table was built from the quote line `fetch.py quote` prints: id, mid, spread, volume, scheduled end, condition and token ids, eligibility. The line carried nothing about how YES settles. The desk's words for the two markets came from their titles, "Will Bitcoin reach $80,000 in November?" and "Will Bitcoin dip to $75,000 in November?", and from the 04:40Z snapshot's columns, which hold the same fields. The `description` field of the same Gamma record, 791 and 787 characters, was in the raw body the quote had saved and hashed; nobody printed it, so nobody read it. The FOMC markets of the same view were described correctly because the desk already knew the event; the Bitcoin markets were the first whose rule the desk did not know, and the gap showed at once.

**Why it matters (arithmetic).** For a driftless lognormal price the chance of touching a level within a window is twice the chance of ending beyond it (the reflection principle; `pm_lib.touch_probability`, tested against the closed form and a one-minute simulation). A method built for "ends above 80,000" would have read a probability about half of the right one and, at a departure threshold of 0.10, would very likely have logged a call against the market with the market right and the desk wrong by construction. The market's 0.735 was the number the desk was about to call "too high" with a terminal-probability method; a touch method on the same inputs gives a figure near it or above it.

**What the market knew that the desk did not.** Only its own rules. Every Polymarket market's resolution text is on the record the quote already fetched. The information was not missing; it was unread, because the quote's output presented the market as a number and an end date and the desk accepted that as a description.

**Changes (built and tested 2026-11-04):**

1. *The rules before the number.* `pm-view/fetch.py quote` prints, under each quote line, the market's title and the first 400 characters of its own resolution text, and records the description's SHA-256 and length in `data/manifest.json` (the text itself is printed, never written to a committed file: Polymarket's verdict is `derived`). The desk reads how YES settles before it writes a proposition or a probability. `pm_lib.rules_excerpt`, tested.
2. *`settles_on` in the ledger.* A new ledger column between `proposition` and `due_on`: the desk's one-sentence restatement of the market's resolution rule, naming the source and the measured level or date. `pm_lib.call_violations` refuses a call whose `settles_on` is missing, under twenty characters, carries no number, or merely repeats the proposition; `tests/test_ledger_discipline.py` holds the committed ledger to it and checks that the quote a call rests on recorded the description's hash, so the rule was fetched before the desk wrote.
3. *The view's table carries the rule.* From the next view, each market's row states what settles YES in the desk's words, beside its title; a market whose rule the desk cannot restate from the record is not described at all.
4. *The right method for a touch market.* `pm_lib.touch_probability(spot, barrier, sigma, days)` (first passage of a driftless lognormal price, one-minute monitoring treated as continuous, 365-day year) and `pm_lib.realised_vol(closes)` (close-to-close, annualised) are built and tested on synthetic data: closed form, symmetry of up and down barriers at equal log distance, monotone in volatility and time, and a seeded one-minute Monte Carlo of 40,000 paths within three standard errors. Not yet run on a real series: the Friday view runs it, under the FRED series' conditions (no observation committed; the basis sentence says daily Coinbase closes understate an intraday Binance touch; zero drift by construction, so any drift the desk believes in is a reason it must state).

**The rule.** A market is described from its resolution text, never from its title or from the quote's numbers. The quote prints that text so the step cannot be skipped; the ledger refuses a call that does not restate it.

## V1-2026-10-23: the 28 October hike that did not come (written 2026-10-30)

**The call.** Made 2026-10-23 on data to 2026-10-21: the FOMC raises the range by 25 bp on 28 October, confidence 0.70. Outcome: held at 3.75–4.00% by a 10–2 vote, two dissents for a hike ([`outcomes.csv`](outcomes.csv), scored 2026-10-29). Brier 0.49 for the desk against 0.05 for the market-anchored baseline, which put the hike at 0.21 ([`SKILL.md`](SKILL.md)).

**The desk's basis, as logged.** Four reasons: September's hike was unanimous and the statement said the Committee "will deliver price stability"; the September SEP median of 4.1% for end-2026 implied one more hike in two meetings; "the 3-month bill sits 37 bp above the effective funds rate, which is roughly one hike at the next meeting plus part of another"; no financial-stress reason to wait. The downside case named was a weak labour report or a credit event, not the thing that happened: the Committee waited for more information.

**What the baseline knew that the desk did not (observed).** The baseline reads the 4-week bill, which on 2026-10-21 matured 21 days after the meeting and carried the October decision and little else: 3.92% against funds at 3.88%, a 4 bp spread, 21% of a hike once scaled to the post-meeting part of the bill's life ([`baselines.csv`](baselines.csv), inputs column). The desk read the 3-month bill: 4.25% against 3.88%, 37 bp. The two instruments disagreed by a factor of three on the same meeting, and the desk did not notice because it did not look at the shorter one.

**Why the 3-month reading was wrong on its own terms (observed, arithmetic).** The 3-month bill's life held two meetings, 28 October (7 days out, 84 of 91 days after it) and 9 December (49 days out, 42 of 91 days after it). A full 25 bp hike at both would have lifted the bill's average rate over its life by 0.25 × 84/91 + 0.25 × 42/91 = 34.6 bp. The spread was 37 bp. So even the most hawkish policy path the bill could express fell short of the spread: at least 2 bp of it, and in practice far more, was premium (term, bill supply, the bill-funds basis), not expectations. Read against the 4-week bill's 21% for October, the 37 bp would have needed a December hike probability of 2.8, which is not a probability. The phrase "one hike plus part of another" was never a possible decomposition. The arithmetic is now `baseline.py::max_policy_spread`, with the V1 numbers as its test.

**The basis the desk ignored (observed).** The 3-month constant-maturity yield sat above the effective rate for the whole of 2026 before any hike was priced: a median of 3 to 8 bp from January to May, rising to 17 bp in June and 23 to 24 bp in July and August as the September hike came into view. The 4-week bill's spread over the same months ran from −4 to +1 bp. The desk's reading attributed the entire 3-month spread to policy; a glance at either series' history would have shown the longer bill carrying a premium the shorter one did not.

**Why 0.70 and not the SEP's 0.50 (inferred).** The SEP's "one more hike in two meetings" is, with no further information, 0.50 for October. The desk raised it to 0.70 on the unanimity of the September vote and the statement's wording, both of which were also consistent with a Committee that had done enough and would say so in December. The market's 0.21 was treated as a number to beat rather than as evidence to explain. A call against the market can be right, but it needs a stated reason why the market is wrong; the basis had none. The miss is a reading error, not bad luck: the information needed to read 0.2 to 0.3 was in the same data file the view was built from.

**What was right.** The year-end calls V2 to V5 were made with the forward curve, not against it (baseline 0.66, 0.59, 0.95, 0.70 against the desk's 0.65, 0.60, 0.75, 0.65), and the scoring and baseline machinery did what it was built for: the miss is public, dated and scored against the market, and the 28 October scorer fix kept it from being scored a day early on the wrong row.

**Changes (built and tested 2026-10-30):**

1. *Baseline before confidence.* For every call made on or after 2026-10-30 the baseline row is computed first (`baseline.py --asof <view date> --ids <id> --dry-run` on the draft row) and committed with the call. A confidence more than 0.20 from the baseline must name the baseline in the basis and say in one testable sentence what the market is missing. Enforced on the committed files by [`../desk-view/tests/test_ledger_discipline.py`](../desk-view/tests/test_ledger_discipline.py).
2. *Read the nearest instrument.* The next meeting is read off the bill that matures soonest after it; a spread on a longer bill is decomposed across every meeting inside its life and compared with `max_policy_spread`; whatever exceeds that bound is premium and is said to be. The historical bill-funds basis over the preceding months is quoted beside the reading.
3. *Brier against the baseline every week.* Each desk view reports the desk's and the baseline's Brier scores and the count of scored calls from `SKILL.md`, in the section that reviews the calls, so that the comparison is never only in this folder.
4. *Downside cases name the no-change path.* For a policy call, "the Committee waits" is always one of the written downside cases; it is the most common way a hike call fails.

**Still unknown.** Whether the pause was the end of the cycle or a wait: the two dissents and the absence of lag language say wait, the 2-year's 14 bp fall says end. V3 (flatter curve by year-end) depends on it and is on the wrong side at +38 bp. One scored call says nothing about calibration; the rules above are what one miss can honestly change. A limit of the method: the policy baseline reads the 4-week bill, so it exists only for a meeting inside that bill's life; a call on 9 December made today has no baseline row yet, which is one more reason the desk logged none.
