## 1. The question, the sources and the test object

**Decision: the longshot spread premium at the horizon, tested on Study 013's own rows.** The question:

> **On the snapshot of record at 24 hours before resolution, over the binary Polymarket markets that enter Study 013's sample, is the relative quoted spread (best ask less best bid, divided by the mid) wider for longshots (mid below 0.10) than for markets priced near even (mid in [0.40, 0.60]), by the margin the venue's order-book literature reports for open markets, and what does that spread cost a trader who would fade the compression Study 013 tests for?**

This is the fourth of the scope's research questions as the agenda restated it on 2026-11-01 (microstructure: spreads, depth, fees, resolution timing and resolution-rule risk, and what they cost a trader) and, through its secondary reading, the start of the fifth (a systematic strategy built only from 1–4). It is chosen by what the misses taught: Study 013's liquidity floor (spread ≤ 0.10, volume ≥ 10,000 USDC at the horizon) was fixed on 2026-11-02 with no measurement of the spreads markets carry at that horizon; the one scored reading of the desk's view (6 November, a 75,000 touch leg) stood at 0.0154 against a market of 0.0056, a longshot price where the spread, not the mid, decides what a position costs; and the view's two weekly quotes print a mid and a spread for seven markets and nothing systematic is known about either.

**Sources.**

- **[DU26]** Dubach, Philipp D., "The Anatomy of a Decentralized Prediction Market: Microstructure Evidence from the Polymarket Order Book", arXiv:2604.24366v2 (v1 2026-04-27, v2 2026-05-14), read from the arXiv HTML on 2026-11-16 18:2xZ (the abstract, the introduction's comparison with equities, sections 3.4, 4.3 and 5.1 to 5.2; Table 1's per-decile figures are not in the HTML text and were not read). Data: a tick-level archive of the public order-book feed, 2026-02-21 to 2026-04-15, joined to the on-chain trade record over a 28-day window (2026-02-28 to 2026-03-27); a pre-registered stratified panel of 600 markets (a top-100 stratum by on-chain USDC volume, 4.56 to 96.0 million, and a random stratum; seed committed before computation). What it reports as stylized fact SF1 (quoted or restated from its text): quoted half-spread in basis points of mid, binned by per-market mean mid into deciles, "Median spread is ∼400 bps in the central [0.4, 0.6] range and climbs to 1,300–1,800 bps for markets trading below 0.10"; "the low-probability side is wider than the high-probability side"; the paper reads the magnitude "less like the classical risk-love or misperception story and more like a liquidity-provision constraint: low-probability binary contracts have a bounded upside and an asymmetric downside for the maker". Its introduction puts the median quoted half-spread of the central decile at about 200 bps. The object is the market's time-average over a 28-day window while open, in markets selected by volume; it is not the spread at a fixed horizon before resolution and not a sample of every market that resolves. SF8 (a cross-sectional depth profile explained by duration, price level and volume, with no residual time-to-close effect) is noted and not tested here.
- **[CR26]** and **[WA26]** as read for Study 013 ([QUESTION.md](../013-polymarket-calibration-forward/QUESTION.md) §1; not re-read): the compression of prices towards 0.50 that Study 013 tests, which the secondary reading of this study prices against the half-spread. [WA26]'s slope b = 1.112 implies a compression of about 1.4 probability points at a price of 0.05 and about 1.1 points at 0.60; [CR26]'s interior bins show 5.9 to 7.3 points.
- Other papers returned by the arXiv search of 2026-11-16 18:20Z (query: Polymarket with spread, liquidity or microstructure; 32 results, 15 titles read) and not read beyond their abstracts: arXiv:2606.04217v2 (Qin and Yang, the on-chain trade archive; "Gibbs spread negatively predicts" Brier scores, a trade-based measure, not the quoted spread), arXiv:2607.26245v1 (Young; "one-tick top-of-book spreads" on the 15-minute Bitcoin markets), arXiv:2605.00864v1 (Cheng, Yang and Zou; NBA in-game books, depth of 14.8 shares on average for combinatorial opportunities). None gives a figure for the quoted spread at a horizon before resolution across resolved markets; none is added to the agenda's register until read.

**Primary trial M1.** The sample: every market in Study 013's data-step sample for its 24-hour horizon ([PREREGISTRATION.md](../013-polymarket-calibration-forward/PREREGISTRATION.md) §5.2 and §6.3: binary, order book, two tokens, spread ≤ 0.10, 0.01 ≤ p ≤ 0.99, volume ≥ 10,000 USDC at the snapshot of record, `closedTime` in [2026-11-04, 2026-12-30)), **less every market of the two events the desk's weekly view follows** (Polymarket events 1292829, the November Bitcoin touch markets, and 770450, the December FOMC decision; [`pm-view/events.csv`](../../pm-view/events.csv)), because the desk has read their books and spreads by hand at the weekly views since 3 November. For each market, **mid** is the midpoint of best bid and best ask of the first listed outcome token on the snapshot-of-record row (Study 013's p), **s** is (best ask − best bid) / mid on the same row, with a spread below the market's `orderPriceMinTickSize` floored at the tick and counted. The **longshot bin** is 0.01 ≤ mid < 0.10 and the **central bin** 0.40 ≤ mid ≤ 0.60; the high-probability bin 0.90 < mid ≤ 0.99 is reported beside them (the asymmetry [DU26] describes) with no decision weight. The decision statistic is

  Δ = mean(ln s | longshot) − mean(ln s | central),

with a standard error clustered by parent event, and decides on **H1: Δ > 0** against **H0: Δ ≤ 0** (no relative premium for longshots at the horizon). The published premium in these units is ln(1300 / 400) = 1.18 to ln(1800 / 400) = 1.50; the pre-registration fixes whether the pass bar is Δ > 0 alone or Δ at least half the published premium (the second says the premium survives at the horizon at a size that matters; the first only that it exists), and says why.

**Why a log difference of means and not a difference of medians or a decile curve.** The sd of ln s is bounded on each bin by the sample's own cap and tick (section 2), so the power is computable from counts alone with no spread read; a median difference needs a density at the median, which cannot be stated before the data are read, and a ten-decile curve is a description, not a decision. The median by decile is reported beside Δ for the reader, against [DU26]'s Figure 1, with no decision weight.

**Secondary reading (no decision weight, pre-declared so it is not chosen after the fact).** For each bin, the median half-spread in probability points (s · mid / 2) beside the compression Study 013 tests for at that price under [WA26]'s slope (about 1.4 points at 0.05, 1.1 at 0.60) and [CR26]'s interior bins (5.9 to 7.3 points). A fade of the compression that enters at the ask and holds to resolution pays the half-spread once; the table says in which bins the published compression clears it and in which it is inside the quote. Fees are not modelled (the row carries `feesEnabled`, not a rate; the venue's published fee schedule is cited beside the table). This is scope question 5's first number, not a strategy: nothing is sized or traded.

## 2. Power, from counts only ([`tools/power_pm014.txt`](../../tools/power_pm014.txt))

The independent counts are Study 013's clean counts per 4 and 8 weeks, read from [`tools/power_pm013.txt`](../../tools/power_pm013.txt) (markets 38,983 / 77,967; distinct parent events 22,347 / 44,694; the systematic one-in-five day sample of 2026-08-01 to 2026-10-31 scaled to the window), because this study reads the same rows. The bin shares at the horizon are not known without reading prices, so the table prints a grid (longshot share 0.10, 0.25, 0.50; central share 0.02, 0.05, 0.10) and the decision row is the thinnest: **8 weeks, distinct events, longshot share 0.10, central share 0.02 (n₁ = 4,469, n₂ = 894)**.

The sd of ln s on each bin is bounded by Popoviciu's inequality from the sample's spread cap (0.10) and the smallest tick the venue uses (0.001; most markets 0.01, the wider bound is kept): longshot bin s ∈ [0.01, 10], sd ≤ 3.454; central bin s ∈ [0.00167, 0.25], sd ≤ 2.505. MDE = K · sqrt(sd₁²/n₁ + sd₂²/n₂) with K = 2.842 (the programme's convention) and 2.486 (one-sided 5% at 80% power). On the decision row **MDE = 0.280 in log units, ratio 0.24 to the smaller published premium (1.18)**; at K = 2.486, 0.245 and 0.21. The thinnest 4-week row gives 0.396 and 0.34. These are upper bounds on the MDE: the realised sd of ln s is smaller than its range, and the realised power is computed with the result and reported.

The cross-check on the newest kept snapshot (2026-11-16, fields read: `enableOrderBook`, `outcomes`, `endDate`, `volumeNum`, `eventIds`; no price field): 162,998 rows, 95,839 binary order-book markets with a scheduled end in [2026-11-17, 2026-12-30), of which 891 markets in 484 events are already at the volume floor, a lower bound on the window's clean count (markets listed later and markets that reach the floor later are not in it; the monthly rate of the 013 count, about 8,700 clean markets a month, is the figure the decision row uses).

## 3. What the author knows before choosing, and the data plan

- **What has been read, and by whom.** No bid, ask, spread or mid of any windowed market has been read for this study. The desk's weekly view has read, by hand and on quote days (3, 6 and 13 November), the books of ten markets in the two followed events, and the event listings of 13 November carried best bid and ask for 34 legs; every market of those two events is excluded from the decision sample above. Study 013's engine, in its data step after 2027-01-20, will read the same snapshot-of-record rows (mid, spread, volume) to apply its own floor; this study's data step reads 013's data-step output rows, never the snapshots directly and never `outcome.jsonl`, so the two studies read one record and this one adds nothing to what 013 reads.
- **Timeline.** The sample is defined by `closedTime`, which is known only at resolution, so no row of this study exists before Study 013's data step runs (after the scoring deadline, 2027-01-20 00:00Z). Nothing is computed before then; the pre-registration, its design review and the engine's code and tests on synthetic rows are the work until then. The daily snapshots already on the clock are the only data collection.
- **The pre-registration will fix:** the pass bar (Δ > 0 or half the published premium); the clustering unit (parent event, as 013); the treatment of a locked or crossed book (bid ≥ ask: excluded and counted, or floored); the tick floor; the high-probability bin's report; the per-decile table's bins (ten, as [DU26]); the stop rules inherited from 013 (fewer than 42 serving days, a run of 3 gap days); the exact rows read from 013's output and their hashes; the separate-session design review before the commit, as 013.

## 4. Why not the alternatives

- **Cross-venue divergence (scope question 2):** parked by the owner's rule until a second venue's terms allow it.
- **Information speed (question 3):** needs intraday quotes around scheduled news; the daily snapshot cannot see it, and a tick archive is a new collector with its own terms question.
- **Depth (question 4's other half):** the snapshot carries `liquidityNum`, a venue figure of unknown construction, not a book; a depth test would need the CLOB book at the horizon for every windowed market, a fetch the collector does not make.
- **Resolution-rule risk (question 4):** the moved-end count is pre-declared above as a description; a decision test needs a published base rate the search of 2026-11-16 did not find in a readable form (arXiv:2609.15368v1 reports 1,604 disputes among 159,447 proposals on chain, a population figure for a different object, read from its abstract only).
- **The same spread question measured now, on open markets relative to the scheduled end:** available today, but the scheduled end moves (the 14 November post-mortem "A window the venue moved and the method did not"), and a result on open markets would read Study 013's own rows before its window closes; the horizon before `closedTime` keeps the two studies on one record and this one unread until then.

## 5. What this note does not do

It fixes no pre-registration, reads no spread, bid, ask or mid of a windowed market, writes no engine and makes no call. It commits the question, the sources read, the test object and the power ratio from counts only, so that a reader can see that each was on record before any spread was read.

## Question gate

- Power ratio: 0.24 of the published longshot spread premium (MDE 0.280 in log units against [DU26]'s ln(1300/400) = 1.18, the smaller end of SF1) on the decision row of tools/power_pm014.txt: 8 w, distinct parent events, longshot share 0.10, central share 0.02, K = 2.842, n₁ = 4,469, n₂ = 894; the sd bounds come from the sample's spread cap and tick, so the MDE is an upper bound.
- Persistence: the premium [DU26] describes is a maker's inventory-risk cost on a bounded-upside, asymmetric-downside contract, not a mispricing that a taker removes by trading, so it should persist as long as makers bear that risk; at a day before resolution it may instead collapse, because most longshots are then settled in all but name and quoted at the tick on both sides, or widen, because makers withdraw before a known resolution, and either is a result on the record; the rule fails against its benchmark (equal relative spread across bins) if the venue's tick alone sets both bins' quotes at the horizon.
- Data terms: Polymarket's Terms of Use effective 2026-08-11, read on 2026-11-01 (tools/pm_data_terms.md, verdict `derived`): aggregates computed by the desk may be published with attribution, no raw response is committed; the snapshot rows and 013's data-step rows are withheld (git-ignored, hashed in manifests) and a reader regenerates them by running the committed collector and engines against the public API; [DU26]'s data are not used; the verdict is re-read before the pre-registration.
- Unmined: the spread at 24 h and 168 h before `closedTime` of markets resolving in [2026-11-04, 2026-12-30) has been read by no one: [DU26] reads 2026-02-21 to 2026-04-15 on open markets' time averages, Study 013 reads the price and the outcome of these rows and not the spread as a test object, and the desk's hand readings are confined to the two excluded events; months before November 2026 are not a decision row, and any historical slice is labelled a replication.
