## A Brier figure rounded twice (written 2026-11-11, 06:15Z run)

**What was wrong (observed).** The 6 November desk view ([`../desk-view/DESK_VIEW.md`](../desk-view/DESK_VIEW.md), section 4) reported the week's Brier comparison as "1 scored call; desk 0.490, baseline 0.046, skill −0.445". The record ([`baselines.csv`](baselines.csv): V1 priced at 0.2133 by the bill-implied method, scored a miss in [`outcomes.csv`](outcomes.csv)) gives a baseline Brier of 0.2133² = 0.04549689, which is 0.045 at three decimals, not 0.046. The desk figure and the skill figure were right (0.49 and −0.44450311). The wrong digit stood on the public page from 06:26Z on 6 November to this run, five days. Shown before any change on fd7ca10: `skill_summary` through the three gated readers prints 0.04549689, `f"{0.0455:.3f}"` prints 0.045 (Python rounds the binary value, which lies below the half), and the page's 0.046 is what a round-half-up by hand of the text "0.0455" gives. The 00:15Z run saw the line as inconsistent (0.046 − 0.490 is −0.444, not the printed −0.445) and listed it; the inconsistency was real but the wrong figure was the baseline, not the skill.

**Why it was there.** The view's section 4 is prose typed by the desk at the Friday run; its numbers come from `data/numbers_<date>.json` and the page says so, but the Brier line was copied from `SKILL.md`, which prints four decimals, and the desk rounded the text to three by hand. SKILL.md's 0.0455 is itself a rounding of 0.04549689; rounding it again moved the digit. Nothing read the line back: README rule "Brier every week" (30 October, rule 3 of the V1 post-mortem) said each view reports the figures and no tool or test held the sentence, so the rule was prose with a check nowhere, the class the mechanical search of 6 November was built for, in a folder the search never reached (`desk-view/` was named as untagged "until a rule changes there"). The 18:15Z run of 10 November had just filed the lesson that a value typed by hand is a reading to be held against its record (Study 003's `--holding`); this is the same lesson on a page.

**What changed (built and tested 06:1x–06:3xZ).** `baseline.view_brier_clause(summary, decimals=3)` renders the clause from the unrounded summary (a minus sign as the view writes it, U+2212; "no scored call with a baseline yet" when the count is zero). `baseline.view_brier_violations(text, summary)` is the gate: exactly one line of the page carries the lead "Brier against the market-anchored baseline"; while the record has a scored call with a baseline the line carries `<n> scored call(s); desk <p>, baseline <p>, skill <s>` with the record's count and each figure equal to the record's figure at the decimals the line itself prints, all three alike (two decimals throughout is held at two; a skill one digit off, a count of two, a hyphen for the minus, mixed precision, a missing clause, a missing line and two lines each refused or admitted by name); with no scored call the line carries no figure. `baseline.record_summary()` reads the three records through their gates, the readers that write SKILL.md. [`../tools/check_view_brier.py`](../tools/check_view_brier.py) applies the gate to the current view in `make lint` and CI (`FAIL … fix the page, never the record`, each sentence on its own line, then the clause to carry; `--print` renders the clause for the next view so the figures are copied from the tool; `--selftest`, 14 cases). [`../desk-view/tests/test_view_brier.py`](../desk-view/tests/test_view_brier.py) (13 tests) holds the renderer, the alterations and the committed page against the committed records. On the unfixed page the tool refused with one sentence, `baseline: the view prints 0.046, the record's 0.04549689 is 0.045 at 3 decimals`, and the committed-page test failed on that sentence alone; the page's digit was then corrected with a dated note under the line, and both pass. README: the "Brier every week" bullet points to the new dated section; the desk-view README names the tool and the test. SKILL.md, the three records and the archived views were not touched.

**Rule.** A figure copied onto a page is a reading of its record and is held against it by a tool, at the precision the page prints, from the record's unrounded value; a figure that exists only as rounded text in another file is not a source to round again. A rule that says "each view reports X" has a tool that prints X and a check that reads it back, in the same commit as the rule.

**Still unknown and not done.** The archived 30 October view says "Brier score 0.49 for the desk against 0.05", which the gate would hold at two decimals; archives are not checked (the text is as published and the folder rule forbids edits). `SKILL.md` itself is held current only by the runs' habit of regenerating it with `--summary --dry-run` and comparing; a lint check that the committed SKILL.md equals a regeneration (but for the date) is the next candidate of this class, with the PM view's `SKILL.md` beside it. The page's own claim that every number on it is in `data/numbers_<date>.json` does not cover this line; the line links to SKILL.md instead, and the correction note says where the figures are held.

**Limits (stated).** The gate holds a row's shape against its source, not the settlement's truth: a `kind` of `first-token` on a market that settled NO passes if `y` is 1 and the briers agree with it; the hashes are held to their shape, not re-fetched. `scored_on` is held not before `made_on`, not against the day the row became due (that needs the manifest and is the scorer's path). The register applies the kind-and-`y` test only, not the full gate, because it answers one question (is this id scored); the full gate is at `score.py` and the committed-file test. Both files are empty, so nothing on the public record changed. The search's own limit stands: a reader that builds its frame by hand from the CSV bypasses `read_scored`; the committed-file test is the backstop.

**The rule.** A record is held at every reader against the row it scores, and a reader that asks a file one narrow question (which ids, which y is non-empty) has to hold the whole row first, because each narrow question has an answer for any text. A copy of a number kept in a scored row for convenience is held equal to its source as text, or the summary and the table beside it will one day print two figures for one call. A test fixture is a row the record's own gate would admit; a fixture the gate would refuse is a defect in the test, found only when the reader is given the gate.

**The desk's basis, as logged.** Four reasons: September's hike was unanimous and the statement said the Committee "will deliver price stability"; the September SEP median of 4.1% for end-2026 implied one more hike in two meetings; "the 3-month bill sits 37 bp above the effective funds rate, which is roughly one hike at the next meeting plus part of another"; no financial-stress reason to wait. The downside case named was a weak labour report or a credit event, not the thing that happened: the Committee waited for more information.

**What the baseline knew that the desk did not (observed).** The baseline reads the 4-week bill, which on 2026-10-21 matured 21 days after the meeting and carried the October decision and little else: 3.92% against funds at 3.88%, a 4 bp spread, 21% of a hike once scaled to the post-meeting part of the bill's life ([`baselines.csv`](baselines.csv), inputs column). The desk read the 3-month bill: 4.25% against 3.88%, 37 bp. The two instruments disagreed by a factor of three on the same meeting, and the desk did not notice because it did not look at the shorter one.

**Why the 3-month reading was wrong on its own terms (observed, arithmetic).** The 3-month bill's life held two meetings, 28 October (7 days out, 84 of 91 days after it) and 9 December (49 days out, 42 of 91 days after it). A full 25 bp hike at both would have lifted the bill's average rate over its life by 0.25 × 84/91 + 0.25 × 42/91 = 34.6 bp. The spread was 37 bp. So even the most hawkish policy path the bill could express fell short of the spread: at least 2 bp of it, and in practice far more, was premium (term, bill supply, the bill-funds basis), not expectations. Read against the 4-week bill's 21% for October, the 37 bp would have needed a December hike probability of 2.8, which is not a probability. The phrase "one hike plus part of another" was never a possible decomposition. The arithmetic is now `baseline.py::max_policy_spread`, with the V1 numbers as its test.

**The basis the desk ignored (observed).** The 3-month constant-maturity yield sat above the effective rate for the whole of 2026 before any hike was priced: a median of 3 to 8 bp from January to May, rising to 17 bp in June and 23 to 24 bp in July and August as the September hike came into view. The 4-week bill's spread over the same months ran from −4 to +1 bp. The desk's reading attributed the entire 3-month spread to policy; a glance at either series' history would have shown the longer bill carrying a premium the shorter one did not.

**Why 0.70 and not the SEP's 0.50 (inferred).** The SEP's "one more hike in two meetings" is, with no further information, 0.50 for October. The desk raised it to 0.70 on the unanimity of the September vote and the statement's wording, both of which were also consistent with a Committee that had done enough and would say so in December. The market's 0.21 was treated as a number to beat rather than as evidence to explain. A call against the market can be right, but it needs a stated reason why the market is wrong; the basis had none. The miss is a reading error, not bad luck: the information needed to read 0.2 to 0.3 was in the same data file the view was built from.

**What was right.** The year-end calls V2 to V5 were made with the forward curve, not against it (baseline 0.66, 0.59, 0.95, 0.70 against the desk's 0.65, 0.60, 0.75, 0.65), and the scoring and baseline machinery did what it was built for: the miss is public, dated and scored against the market, and the 28 October scorer fix kept it from being scored a day early on the wrong row.

**Changes (built and tested 2026-10-30):**

1. *Baseline before confidence.* For every call made on or after 2026-10-30 the baseline row is computed first (`baseline.py --asof <view date> --ids <id> --dry-run` on the draft row) and committed with the call. A confidence more than 0.20 from the baseline must name the baseline in the basis and say in one testable sentence what the market is missing. Enforced on the committed files by [`../desk-view/tests/test_ledger_discipline.py`](../desk-view/tests/test_ledger_discipline.py).
2. *Read the nearest instrument.* The next meeting is read off the bill that matures soonest after it; a spread on a longer bill is decomposed across every meeting inside its life and compared with `max_policy_spread`; whatever exceeds that bound is premium and is said to be. The historical bill-funds basis over the preceding months is quoted beside the reading.
3. *Brier against the baseline every week.* Each desk view reports the desk's and the baseline's Brier scores and the count of scored calls from `SKILL.md`, in the section that reviews the calls, so that the comparison is never only in this folder.
4. *Downside cases name the no-change path.* For a policy call, "the Committee waits" is always one of the written downside cases; it is the most common way a hike call fails.

**Still unknown.** Whether the pause was the end of the cycle or a wait: the two dissents and the absence of lag language say wait, the 2-year's 14 bp fall says end. V3 (flatter curve by year-end) depends on it and is on the wrong side at +38 bp. One scored call says nothing about calibration; the rules above are what one miss can honestly change. A limit of the method: the policy baseline reads the 4-week bill, so it exists only for a meeting inside that bill's life; a call on 9 December made today has no baseline row yet, which is one more reason the desk logged none.
