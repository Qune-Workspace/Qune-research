# Post-mortems of the desk's calls

One entry per scored miss or near-miss, newest first. Each ends in a rule, a test or a changed procedure, and a line in [`../BUILD_LOG.md`](../BUILD_LOG.md). The ledger is never edited; the lesson goes here and into the method.

## V1-2026-10-23: the 28 October hike that did not come (written 2026-10-30)

**The call.** Made 2026-10-23 on data to 2026-10-21: the FOMC raises the range by 25 bp on 28 October, confidence 0.70. Outcome: held at 3.75–4.00% by a 10–2 vote, two dissents for a hike ([`outcomes.csv`](outcomes.csv), scored 2026-10-29). Brier 0.49 for the desk against 0.05 for the market-anchored baseline, which put the hike at 0.21 ([`SKILL.md`](SKILL.md)).

**The desk's basis, as logged.** Four reasons: September's hike was unanimous and the statement said the Committee "will deliver price stability"; the September SEP median of 4.1% for end-2026 implied one more hike in two meetings; "the 3-month bill sits 37 bp above the effective funds rate, which is roughly one hike at the next meeting plus part of another"; no financial-stress reason to wait. The downside case named was a weak labour report or a credit event, not the thing that happened: the Committee waited for more information.

**What the baseline knew that the desk did not (observed).** The baseline reads the 4-week bill, which on 2026-10-21 matured 21 days after the meeting and carried the October decision and little else: 3.92% against funds at 3.88%, a 4 bp spread, 21% of a hike once scaled to the post-meeting part of the bill's life ([`baselines.csv`](baselines.csv), inputs column). The desk read the 3-month bill: 4.25% against 3.88%, 37 bp. The two instruments disagreed by a factor of three on the same meeting, and the desk did not notice because it did not look at the shorter one.

**Why the 3-month reading was wrong on its own terms (observed, arithmetic).** The 3-month bill's life held two meetings, 28 October (7 days out, 84 of 91 days after it) and 9 December (49 days out, 42 of 91 days after it). A full 25 bp hike at both would have lifted the bill's average rate over its life by 0.25 × 84/91 + 0.25 × 42/91 = 34.6 bp. The spread was 37 bp. So even the most hawkish policy path the bill could express fell short of the spread: at least 2 bp of it, and in practice far more, was premium (term, bill supply, the bill-funds basis), not expectations. Read against the 4-week bill's 21% for October, the 37 bp would have needed a December hike probability of 2.8, which is not a probability. The phrase "one hike plus part of another" was never a possible decomposition. The arithmetic is now `baseline.py::max_policy_spread`, with the V1 numbers as its test.

**The basis the desk ignored (observed).** The 3-month constant-maturity yield sat above the effective rate for the whole of 2026 before any hike was priced: a median of 3 to 8 bp from January to May, rising to 17 bp in June and 23 to 24 bp in July and August as the September hike came into view. The 4-week bill's spread over the same months ran from −4 to +1 bp. The desk's reading attributed the entire 3-month spread to policy; a glance at either series' history would have shown the longer bill carrying a premium the shorter one did not.

**Why 0.70 and not the SEP's 0.50 (inferred).** The SEP's "one more hike in two meetings" is, with no further information, 0.50 for October. The desk raised it to 0.70 on the unanimity of the September vote and the statement's wording, both of which were also consistent with a Committee that had done enough and would say so in December. The market's 0.21 was treated as a number to beat rather than as evidence to explain. A call against the market can be right, but it needs a stated reason why the market is wrong; the basis had none. The miss is a reading error, not bad luck: the information needed to read 0.2 to 0.3 was in the same data file the view was built from.

**What was right.** The year-end calls V2 to V5 were made with the forward curve, not against it (baseline 0.66, 0.59, 0.95, 0.70 against the desk's 0.65, 0.60, 0.75, 0.65), and the scoring and baseline machinery did what it was built for: the miss is public, dated and scored against the market, and the 28 October scorer fix kept it from being scored a day early on the wrong row.

**Changes (built and tested 2026-10-30):**

1. *Baseline before confidence.* For every call made on or after 2026-10-30 the baseline row is computed first (`baseline.py --asof <view date> --ids <id> --dry-run` on the draft row) and committed with the call. A confidence more than 0.20 from the baseline must name the baseline in the basis and say in one testable sentence what the market is missing. Enforced on the committed files by [`../desk-view/tests/test_ledger_discipline.py`](../desk-view/tests/test_ledger_discipline.py).
2. *Read the nearest instrument.* The next meeting is read off the bill that matures soonest after it; a spread on a longer bill is decomposed across every meeting inside its life and compared with `max_policy_spread`; whatever exceeds that bound is premium and is said to be. The historical bill-funds basis over the preceding months is quoted beside the reading.
3. *Brier against the baseline every week.* Each desk view reports the desk's and the baseline's Brier scores and the count of scored calls from `SKILL.md`, in the section that reviews the calls, so that the comparison is never only in this folder.
4. *Downside cases name the no-change path.* For a policy call, "the Committee waits" is always one of the written downside cases; it is the most common way a hike call fails.

**Still unknown.** Whether the pause was the end of the cycle or a wait: the two dissents and the absence of lag language say wait, the 2-year's 14 bp fall says end. V3 (flatter curve by year-end) depends on it and is on the wrong side at +38 bp. One scored call says nothing about calibration; the rules above are what one miss can honestly change. A limit of the method: the policy baseline reads the 4-week bill, so it exists only for a meeting inside that bill's life; a call on 9 December made today has no baseline row yet, which is one more reason the desk logged none.
